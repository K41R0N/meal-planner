# Visual direction proposal

Status: unapproved design work, not a replacement of the app yet. User priority outranks any tool-generated style assignment. Existing product truth is preserved; the old warm-serif skin is not binding after the requested distinct redesign.

## Design authority and scene

A one-handed Android user in a grocery aisle or busy kitchen needs readable quantities and one action. On web, the same person has room to compare recipe cards and adjust a week. This is a task-first product with food character, not a marketing site or a fitness dashboard. Recipe cards apply on both platforms. The working visual name is Mealbook; the meal-planner repository stays central.

## Grounded directions considered

1. **Modern grocer labels:** clean packs and bold product names; cool greens, white, dark ink, short evidence labels. Strong familiar food identification, but could converge on a typical meal-app palette.
2. **Food-photography contact sheets:** confident picture-led scanning with black/white functional type and one selection color. Honest when real dish photos exist; weak if imported thumbnails are mostly creator faces.
3. **Neighborhood produce market:** saturated stall colors, a strong name and readable category signs. Broadly welcoming; category colors must not compete with warning/state colors.
4. **Cooking-club event identity:** energetic community-print type and a compact color system. Memorable but could feel like a social product the scope does not include.
5. **Kitchen textile system:** woven blues and whites, precise repeated rhythm. Familiar at home; the texture must remain inspiration rather than decoration behind operational text.
6. **Shared-table ceramics:** glazes in blue/green and warm food color; rounded, tactile family of surfaces. Characterful but risks becoming a vague mood without a useful card/state grammar.
7. **Contemporary food magazine:** vivid food color against a cool white/Atlantic shell, confident readable sans, food-first decision cards rather than article layouts. The executed Impeccable concept seed selected this candidate index. The working product's standard navigation and controls remain intact.

## Proposed lead: Everyday food, ready for action

Use the food magazine's **type, palette, density and one signature move**, not its article layout. Deep Atlantic `#16425A` anchors navigation and quantities; oyster-blue `#F2F7F8` is the task background; white is the reading/card field. A vivid citrus `#F2B643` belongs to the current action, while a distinct dark green with a text label indicates confirmed ready portions. Error and warning states remain semantically separate. Food photography supplies most of the warmth; no cream-and-Georgia wellness template.

A licensed General Sans workhorse family carries titles, controls and prose. Set quantities in tabular numerals, recipe titles with strong but modest weight, method text generously, and secondary information below the decision. No tiny tracked labels as the universal design motif.

**Signature move:** the same recipe card retains its name, image and position while its visible state band changes from selected → to cook → ready portions → eaten. Handoffs attach a compact portion/plan context to that card, not a new generic panel. Motion only confirms movement into a batch or a state change, about 150–220 ms, with immediate reduced-motion equivalents. The user's next action does not wait for animation.

**First viewport:** labeled navigation; Today's recipe with one large food card and a visible state; the state-matched action in the phone thumb area or beside the card on web; a quiet ready-portions/next-cook summary. A calorie graph, planner settings and warning editor do not lead. Desktop has a supporting week rail and enough room for useful food cards; phone is composed for the hand rather than squeezed.

**Cross-surface reach:** recipe book uses a food-card family, batch planning uses selected cards with concrete portion controls, shop uses plain aisle rows with small recipe contribution labels, and cooking uses a focused step card. Everything is not a decorative card.

**Honest risk:** a food-first system needs honest dish imagery. Many current YouTube thumbnails show the creator, not the cooked food. Use those as source thumbnails; acquire an actual dish photo when permitted/user-supplied, or use an expressive non-photographic fallback. Concept food photos must be labeled illustrative and must not become fake creator photos in the shipping app.

## Actual challenger evaluation

The installed Impeccable tool returned these catalog systems. All were weighed against audience identification and task clarity; Operate-mode restrictions exclude transplanting their layout or controls.

- **Datamatics / barcode field — declined:** wrong association for cooking and too visually forceful. Keep its discipline of precise, stable number columns and high contrast, not barcodes or strobing.
- **Vertical video feed — competitive for the food-first focal card only:** strong audience recognition from YouTube, but a feed and rail of unlabeled glyphs would reduce task clarity. Keep one dominant item and a quick next choice; no autoplay, engagement metrics or full-screen discovery feed.
- **Cassette J-card — declined:** a timed set is relevant to cooking windows, but hand lettering and tape flipping are not clearer food controls. Keep an explicit time/portion capacity, not cassette styling.
- **Dark developer console — declined:** task density is useful, cultural fit is wrong for the kitchen. Keep destructive actions isolated and responsive panel priority, not console colors or jargon.
- **Labanotation — declined:** precise duration is valuable, notation is not familiar grocery/cooking language. Keep active/passive time legible, not a dance score.
- **Tabbed acetate manual — declined:** layered disclosure is useful, but physical folder/acetate simulation adds a new interaction to learn. Keep consistent information depth and clear state hierarchy, not transparent leaves or ornamental tab rails.

These kept disciplines strengthen the lead's clarity without mixing costumes. The alternative **Modern grocer labels** remains a safer, familiar option; **Food-first focus** explores the competitive video focal mechanism with standard buttons. The user can approve, combine or reject the proposal.

## Information and interaction rules

One primary action; no essential hidden flips or gestures. Food identity first, next action second, decision facts third, source/editing fourth. Show unknown time/nutrition honestly. On phone, labeled bottom navigation and minimum 44 px controls; on web, persistent navigation, scan-friendly recipe cards, full keyboard/focus paths. System back returns to the previous context. Use source-preserving edits and local draft recovery. Shopping rows favor speed and quantities over illustration. Recipe cards, detail and cooking share identity but do not share the same density.

The direction remains a proposal until the user approves the experience package. No claims of Awwwards affiliation, award status, user-testing success or world-class achievement.
