# Commercial offer and go-to-market

Updated October 27, 2026. Proposed work; no campaigns, outreach, checkout or public store listing have been created. [Strategy](../../STRATEGY.md) owns the positioning and [subscriptions](subscriptions.md) owns the proposed packaging rules.

## Reach the right first user

Focus on people who already save recipes and try to meal prep toward calorie/protein targets. The hardest work is finding realistic cooking time and making one shop cover useful meals. Demonstrate a specific journey: save a permitted creator recipe, review yield/macros, choose two cooking opportunities, produce a prep week, shop compatible quantities and reuse actual ready food. The promise is a dependable week, not “understands every video.”

Recipe-capture incumbents and goal planners already have paid offers; the [dated benchmark](../research/market/commercial-strategy.md) is evidence of adjacent demand, not our demand. The [six deeper audits](../research/competitors/production-patterns.md) inform onboarding, trust and upsell. Do not treat undocumented competitor capabilities as missing features or claim that a new decision model is a moat.

## Activation before paywall

Guest onboarding should let the person cook or shop a useful recipe before creating a complete profile. Ask for preferences and goals only when they change an action. Show uncertainty and correction costs before asking for trust. A sample licensed recipe is useful; a fabricated nutrition-perfect example is not.

Proposed upsell moments are **requesting a numerical goal-fit week**, **rebalancing an existing week around missed cooking**, or **enabling optional multi-device sync**. Present the actual added outcome and its limitations. Avoid a paywall after the user has spent time correcting an import, blocking export, hiding owned recipes at expiry or disguising per-week billing as monthly pricing. Existing personal-user functionality cannot be reclassified behind a paywall without approval and a migration promise.

The initial price/trial/import-cap proposal is $7.99 monthly / $49.99 annually, a 14-day trial and finite successful assisted-import quotas. These are test variables, not deployed plans. ReciMe's documented five free weekly imports is more generous than the proposed five monthly free imports; if our cap blocks a useful second week, adjust it against measured cost instead of asserting a superior free tier. Source and price uncertainty remain in the market report.

## Channels worth testing first

**Permissioned creators:** Collaborate on a few rights-cleared recipes and show the creator's dishes becoming a week and grocery run. Agree attribution, images, distribution and referral terms. A saved source link is not endorsement or permission. Track activated repeat-week users, not just clicks.

**Task-specific demonstrations:** Short Android share-to-review and cooking/shop walkthroughs with honest extraction corrections. Show ingredient reuse and ready-food handling; do not promise savings without purchase prices or nutrition accuracy without reviewed data.

**Search and content:** Publish original helpful material on batch cooking, recipe-to-list workflows and realistic protein planning after the public web surface is ready. Keep product claims consistent with shipped features; technical preview success does not imply crawler-visible public pages. A public content/catalog SEO implementation is future work, not this documentation pass.

**Community participation:** Recruit only where rules allow, with disclosed affiliation and a specific research task. No scraped outreach lists, mass posting or unsolicited medical/dietary advice. Build referrals after trust and repeat use; do not assume retention from an influencer launch.

Paid acquisition comes later. Define contribution margin, support/import cost and renewal cohorts first. Unavailable competitor ad-spend or revenue data is not a license to invent our customer-acquisition budget.

## Six-week paid pilot

The proposed 30-person pilot begins only after the closed-beta release gates. Screen for an existing recipe-saving and prep habit. During two trial weeks, observe recipe correction burden, successful plan/shop/cook actions, missing-time recovery and whether ready food makes the second week easier. Offer the real monthly price with cancellation and finite import allowance clearly visible.

Predeclare team decision rules: 20 usable first weeks, 12 second weeks, eight actual payments and five first renewals. These are directional pilot gates, not industry benchmarks. Store the eligible participant count, exposure, actual payments, refunds and timing so cancellation or missing data cannot inflate conversion. First renewal around week six depends on actual trial/billing dates.

Define a useful week as a confirmed plan, meaningful shopping activity and at least one completed cooking session. Actual intake is optional, not a condition for commercial activation; forcing logging could undermine the prep job. Measure correction minutes and plan-to-cook completion separately from imports, sessions and downloads. Interview non-returners instead of assuming they dislike the price.

## Instrumentation and commercial decisions

Use consented event names with versions: import attempt/source failure/review completion; first/second useful week; plan revision; shop progress; cooking start/completion; goal-fit residual/unknown state; upgrade view/purchase/cancel/refund/renewal. Include anonymous/cohort IDs only under the agreed policy. Do not send recipe bodies, contact details, dietary restrictions or full source text to generic analytics. Keep operational quota/billing records separate from marketing consent.

A paid user who never cooks a second week signals a capture/activation problem. A repeat cook who does not pay signals packaging or insufficient automation value. A payer who cancels signals retention or trust. Do not respond to all three with more imports or AI branding. Move toward 100 paying users and several renewal cohorts before scaling acquisition; that is a proposed evidence step, not a forecast.

## Responsible claims and support

Store/website copy must identify supported sources and fallback, current versus calculated nutrition, subscription term/limits, privacy/export/deletion and contact support. No medical weight-loss guarantee, allergy-safety guarantee, universal-video support or implied creator sponsorship. Brand/trademark and content-rights review are launch blockers. The operator must provide real refund/billing help and data recovery before monetization, not just a FAQ placeholder.

The [release readiness register](../release/production-readiness.md) controls permission to launch. Pricing scenarios in [the research worksheet](../research/market/pricing-scenarios.csv) are sensitivities, not sales or profit forecasts.
