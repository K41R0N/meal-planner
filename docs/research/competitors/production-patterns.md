# Mealbook commercial production patterns: six-app benchmark

**Evidence date:** 2026-10-27
**Scope:** ReciMe, Eat This Much, MealPrepPro, CookBook, Samsung Food, and Paprika Recipe Manager. This is a synthesis of the six supplied public-evidence audits and their cited original pages only; it adds no fresh research. A documented feature, policy, or help instruction is not a hands-on verification: no account, import, checkout, cancellation, restore, deletion, conflict, or offline flow was executed in the underlying work.

## Decision frame

**Current Mealbook is a local-first personal PWA.** It has no accounts, sync, billing, or goal optimizer. All account, household, cloud, import, entitlement, nutrition-optimization, and marketing capabilities discussed under “Implications” are future options, **not approved features, commitments, or vendor decisions**.

The benchmark supports a simple conclusion: recipe storage and basic planning are increasingly table stakes, while the hard commercial product is trustworthy recurring execution—profile-aware planning, groceries, batch/leftover handling, collaboration, portable data, and comprehensible billing. The evidence does *not* support treating any competitor’s visible platform coverage, app features, or disclosed providers as proof of an equivalent shared architecture.

## 1. Stack evidence versus unknowns

The evidence is uneven and must stay product-specific. **ReciMe** has Android and Apple-device availability; its only concrete implementation lead is an expired third-party reproduction of an iOS hiring listing that names Swift/SwiftUI, Xcode Cloud, REST APIs and Firebase among desired skills. That is directional hiring evidence, not a production inventory and says nothing decisive about Android, backend, parser, payments, or database design ([job listing](https://jaabz.com/jobs/163230-ios-developer-swiftui)).

**Eat This Much** publicly named Cloudflare, analytics/remarketing tools, Stripe, support/email tooling, crash reporting, and Apple HealthKit in a 2020 privacy policy, but did not disclose its current client framework, backend, database, sync protocol, AI, or offline design ([privacy policy](https://www.eatthismuch.com/privacy-policy)). **MealPrepPro**’s 2021 policy names Firebase Authentication and Realtime Database, certain Apple services, Branch, Mailchimp, and Groove; a later help article identifies Stripe for web payments. These are dated processor/service disclosures, not proof of today’s full application architecture ([privacy policy](https://nibbleapps.com/privacy/); [refund help](https://help.mealpreppro.com/help/how-do-i-get-a-refund)).

**CookBook** lists AWS, multiple Firebase products, RevenueCat, Cloudflare, Zendesk, SendinBlue, and payment-related providers. That establishes published provider categories, not data placement, active use per surface, mobile framework, deployment topology, or internal API design ([privacy policy](https://www.cookbook.company/policies/privacy)). **Samsung Food** offers the most specific, but still bounded, disclosures: a closed data-platform role listed Python/SQL, ClickHouse, MySQL, MongoDB, PostgreSQL, Google Cloud data services, Airflow, dbt, and Redash/Kubit; its provider page also names providers for subscriptions, messaging, AI, attribution, image handling, crashes, and infrastructure ([job listing](https://ge.linkedin.com/jobs/view/data-platform-manager-at-samsung-food-4232962154); [service providers](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/18766035615764-Samsung-Food-Service-Providers)). This neither identifies the consumer runtime nor proves all named components are currently active. **Paprika** documents Windows 10 64-bit and .NET Framework 4.6.1+ for its traditional desktop app, which is runtime compatibility—not a statement of cross-platform client language, local database, sync protocol, backend, or cloud provider ([Windows page](https://www.paprikaapp.com/windows/)).

**Implication for Mealbook (recommended architecture boundary):** preserve the current local-first PWA as the factual baseline. If commercial features are later evaluated, define a requirements-led architecture independently: encrypted local data; explicit sync state and retry/error controls; deterministic conflict and deletion semantics; auditability for imports, nutrition revisions, and entitlement changes; and a current subprocessor/retention record. Do not select a backend, database, billing provider, app framework, AI provider, or sync vendor from these disclosures, and do not claim competitor stack parity.

## 2. User profiles, identity, and household collaboration

The audited products generally have **account-led personalization**, but their household models are weak or incomplete. Eat This Much documents saved measurements, diets, nutrition requirements, and editable preferences; its beta linked accounts can copy plans and combine eligible groceries, yet the company warns linked users have full control over one another’s meals and settings ([guide](https://www.eatthismuch.com/how-to/); [linked accounts](https://help.eatthismuch.com/help/family-members-account-linking-beta)). Samsung Food documents diet, avoidances, dislikes, household size, and account-required sharing of recipes, lists, and plans. Its “household” setting is a planning count, rather than evidence of separate household profiles, per-person goals, permissions, or consent boundaries ([account guide](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/18365212922644-Managing-Your-Samsung-Food-Account); [sharing guide](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/18689681101716-Sharing-Collaboration-on-Samsung-Food)).

MealPrepPro documents adding people with an avatar and calorie value, but a 2025 article instructs partners to use the same credentials—despite older terms saying accounts are individual and must not be shared. That is a material unresolved policy/product conflict, not a role model ([add people](https://help.mealpreppro.com/help/add-a-partner-to-your-plan); [sharing article](https://help.mealpreppro.com/help/how-to-share-your-meal-plan-with-your-partner); [terms](https://nibbleapps.com/terms/)). CookBook expressly has no account-linking or family profiles; shared credentials provide full control and separate accounts exchange individual files/QR codes rather than live shared objects ([household help](https://help.cookbookmanager.com/en/article/how-can-i-share-my-whole-cookbook-with-my-partner-or-family-1vynqdy/)). Paprika V3 is a single-account/single-collection construct, while Paprika 4’s separate-family-login claim is only beta-level and leaves roles, ownership, revocation, and conflicts undocumented ([iOS guide](https://www.paprikaapp.com/help/ios/); [Paprika 4 beta](https://www.paprikaapp.com/news/2026/09/28/paprika-4-is-now-in-open-beta/)). ReciMe says accounts should not be shared and does not document household roles ([privacy policy](https://www.recime.app/privacy-policy)).

**Implication for Mealbook (recommended product rule):** should accounts be pursued, treat a personal profile, a household, and a shared plan as different entities. Evaluate invitations, least-privilege roles, separate visibility for health/preferences, shared-grocery edit rules, billing authority, leave/revoke controls, and a shared-data deletion policy. A household-size field, shared password, or collaborator access alone should not be represented as a household model. Until those rules and consent boundaries exist, Mealbook should remain explicitly personal/local-first rather than implying cross-device or multi-person continuity.

## 3. Billing and entitlement lifecycle

The clearest benchmark is not price; it is **state transparency**. ReciMe documents five free imports each week and Plus with a seven-day trial, but current term-to-price mapping, cancellation, refunds, restoration, expiry, and post-lapse handling remain untested or publicly unclear ([free plan](https://recime.app/help/en/articles/11596201-is-there-a-free-version-of-the-app); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.recime.app&hl=en_US&gl=US)). Eat This Much distinguishes free daily planning from Premium weekly generation, groceries/pantry, leftovers, family scaling, saved plans and print/email; it publishes a 14-day trial and a 30-day guarantee, while account deletion does not cancel mobile-store billing ([subscription help](https://help.eatthismuch.com/help/what-do-i-get-for-subscribing); [deletion help](https://help.eatthismuch.com/help/deleting-your-account-4)).

MealPrepPro documents a single seven-day full-access trial and purchase-rail-specific cancellation, refund, and restore help, but lacks a public feature-by-feature gate, confirmed post-expiry access model, or complete Android/web restoration behavior ([trial help](https://help.mealpreppro.com/help/how-does-the-subscription-work); [restore help](https://help.mealpreppro.com/help/how-do-i-restore-my-subscription)). CookBook is comparatively explicit: 20 recipes, 20 smart imports, and two limited lists are free; content above limits becomes read-only at expiry and export remains available. It documents a yearly 14-day trial, immediate monthly billing, route-specific cancellation, and a restore action that relinks a store plan to the signed-in account ([free-limit help](https://help.cookbookmanager.com/en/article/what-happens-when-i-reach-the-free-limits-11q03a/); [restore help](https://help.cookbookmanager.com/en/article/how-do-i-restore-my-cookbook-purchase-1s6opsg/)). Samsung Food publishes a one-per-account, seven-day Food+ trial, U.S.-visible monthly/annual pricing, restore instructions, and store-managed cancellation/refunds; grace, checkout, downgrade, and restored-state behavior remain untested ([subscription manual](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/19522861274388-Managing-Your-Samsung-Food-Subscriptions-Your-User-Manual)). Paprika 4 says premium features will follow its beta but has not published a decision-grade subscription matrix ([beta announcement](https://www.paprikaapp.com/news/2026/09/28/paprika-4-is-now-in-open-beta/)).

**Implication for Mealbook (recommended commercial-control surface):** before any billing launch, define and publish a single entitlement matrix: free limits, trial eligibility, price/term/currency, billing rail, renewal date, cancellation effect, restore rules, refund owner, failed-payment/grace behavior, expiry/read-only behavior, and data/export rights after lapse. Account deletion must be a distinct, plainly explained action from billing cancellation. Retain user-owned data and export access across expiry unless a future policy explicitly says otherwise. These are future design requirements, not an authorization to activate billing.

## 4. Recipe ownership, nutrition, sync, export, and deletion

Personal recipe capture is widely marketed, but **rights, provenance, and portability differ sharply**. ReciMe claims social/YouTube import, editable Cookbooks, cloud sync, and recipe nutrition; its web beta documents library work but says plans and groceries are still mobile-first. Nutrition source, import reliability, source rights, offline behavior, and conflict semantics are unknown ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.recime.app&hl=en_US&gl=US); [web beta help](https://recime.app/help/en/articles/11626084-can-i-access-recime-using-a-computer)). Eat This Much’s clipper requires review before sending URL, HTML, and an optional image; it keeps source attribution and uses time-bounded pending storage. Saved imports become account data, while terms give the company a broad perpetual user-content license ([clipper privacy notice](https://www.eatthismuch.com/recipe-clipper-privacy-policy); [terms](https://www.eatthismuch.com/terms)).

MealPrepPro claims personal/social/website/cookbook imports and nutrition planning, but direct YouTube parsing, import source attribution, nutrition supplier, recipe ownership, offline behavior, and full export coverage are not established ([personal recipe help](https://help.mealpreppro.com/help/add-your-own-recipes); [data export help](https://help.mealpreppro.com/help/how-can-i-request-my-information)). CookBook documents private, same-account cloud collections and bulk recipe export, but exported images are server links—not embedded—and deletion permanently removes them. It estimates nutrition from ingredients with manual edits, but does not identify the underlying ingredient database ([export help](https://help.cookbookmanager.com/en/article/how-do-i-bulk-export-or-back-up-all-of-my-recipes-1o6k9my/); [nutrition help](https://help.cookbookmanager.com/en/article/how-do-i-automatically-calculate-nutritional-information-9f49v7/)).

Samsung Food says users retain ownership but grant an irrevocable, perpetual, transferable, sublicensable license; deletion removes account content while technical/shared copies may persist. It documents several saving methods and web planner sync, not self-serve export, direct YouTube parsing, offline guarantees, nutrition provenance, or conflict resolution ([terms](https://samsungfood.com/policy/terms/); [recipe saving](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/18756427379476-How-to-Save-Recipes-to-Your-Samsung-Food-Recipe-Box)). Paprika offers strongly portable recipe-level imports/exports, locally stored/offline Windows access, and optional cloud sync. But its full-account export, conflict, deletion propagation, data retention, and cross-platform offline behavior are still not confirmed; V4 USDA analysis is a beta claim ([iOS guide](https://www.paprikaapp.com/help/ios/); [Windows page](https://www.paprikaapp.com/windows/); [Paprika 4 beta](https://www.paprikaapp.com/news/2026/09/28/paprika-4-is-now-in-open-beta/)).

**Implication for Mealbook (recommended trust contract):** a later import service should obtain per-import consent; show source/canonical URL, parsing inputs, temporary-storage limit, review-before-save, corrections, error outcomes, and creator attribution. Do not advertise direct YouTube parsing unless it is genuinely supported and tested. Nutrition should expose supplier/version, matching assumptions, servings and batch yield, estimate confidence, manual changes, and recalculation history. Any future cloud edition should offer a documented, self-contained export of structured recipes, sources, images, plans, groceries, pantry, goals, timestamps, and household objects; it should separately state what is not included. Publish deletion scope, local wipe behavior, backup/log retention, and irreversible effects. The existing local-first product should not claim sync, offline parity beyond its current behavior, or data export coverage it does not provide.

## 5. Upsell and marketing patterns

The common value moment is execution, not generic storage: ReciMe gates high-frequency imports; Eat This Much gates weekly automation, groceries, leftovers, and family functions; CookBook uses demonstrated library/import/list quotas; Samsung Food bundles recurring planning, pantry, scans, and AI; Paprika applies recipe/sync limits; MealPrepPro uses a full-access trial. Exact paywall timing, copy, conversion, checkout performance, CAC, and revenue are generally untested or undisclosed. ReciMe alone documents an Ambassador program with link/code attribution and recurring commissions while referred subscribers remain active; its affiliate count is a company claim ([ambassador program](https://recime.app/ambassador-program)). Samsung Food has historical Creator Fund terms, not proof of a current program ([Creator Fund terms](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/17950644133012-The-Samsung-Food-Creator-Fund-Terms-and-Conditions)).

Owned-content patterns exist without performance proof: Eat This Much’s calorie/diet plan pages, CookBook’s comparison article, Samsung Food’s category blog, and Paprika’s release/beta news show content-led acquisition surfaces, while privacy policies may disclose email or remarketing tools. None establishes traffic, rankings, spend, conversion, creator economics, or campaign cadence ([Eat This Much sample plan](https://www.eatthismuch.com/diet-plan/1500-calorie); [CookBook comparison](https://cookbookmanager.com/post/best-recipe-manager-apps); [Samsung Food blog](https://samsungfood.com/blog/); [Paprika news](https://www.paprikaapp.com/news/)).

**Implication for Mealbook (recommended sequencing):** first make local recipe ownership and weekly use measurably useful; then, if a commercial edition is approved, test upgrade propositions around explainable goal-aware planning, batch prep, leftovers, calendar-to-grocery automation, ingredient overlap, and coordinated household work. Any future creator/referral channel should preserve web-to-Android attribution, source credit, opt-outs, clear commission terms, and an import-quality feedback loop. Do not infer marketing performance from public tooling or content existence.

## Decision-useful open boundaries

Across the benchmark, the unresolved areas are consequential: real guest value before account creation; permissions and revocation; enrollment/restore/refund/grace edge cases; full export and backup-purge timing; offline and multi-device conflict behavior; complete nutrition provenance; import accuracy and video parsing; and the actual client/backend/payment/AI stack. These remain unknown unless tested or newly documented. They are precisely the conditions Mealbook should specify before converting its personal local-first PWA into an account, sync, household, optimization, or subscription product.
